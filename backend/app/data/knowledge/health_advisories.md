# General health advisories for air pollution (general guidance, not medical advice)

People with asthma or COPD, children, older adults, pregnant people, and people with heart disease are the most sensitive groups. Anyone with a diagnosed condition should follow their own doctor's action plan.

## Outdoor activity
- Good or Satisfactory air: normal outdoor activity is generally fine; sensitive people may reduce prolonged heavy exertion on the upper end.
- Moderate air: sensitive groups should reduce prolonged or intense outdoor exercise and prefer lighter activity.
- Poor air: sensitive groups should avoid outdoor exercise; everyone else should limit prolonged exertion.
- Very Poor or Severe air: move exercise indoors, minimise time outside, and avoid busy roads.

## Timing
Urban particulate and NO2 levels commonly peak around morning and evening traffic hours, and are often lower in the middle of the day. Ozone behaves differently and tends to peak in the afternoon. Checking the hourly forecast before choosing exercise time is more reliable than a fixed rule.

## Protection
- Well-fitted FFP2 / N95-type respirators reduce exposure to fine particles when outdoors; ordinary cloth or surgical masks give little protection against PM2.5.
- Keep windows closed during pollution peaks; a HEPA air purifier in the room used most helps indoors.
- People with asthma should keep their reliever inhaler accessible, follow their written action plan, and seek medical care if symptoms worsen or do not respond to usual treatment.
- Seek urgent care for severe breathlessness, chest pain, or lips turning blue.
